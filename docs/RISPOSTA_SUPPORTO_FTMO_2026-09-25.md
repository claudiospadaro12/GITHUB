> 1. Demo accounts at another broker
> Yes, the cross-account hedging prohibition also applies if the opposite exposure exists on a demo account with another broker or provider. The rule is not limited to real-money accounts. Opposite positions across different accounts can fall under the Forbidden Trading Practices regardless of whether the accounts are FTMO accounts, accounts with another prop firm, broker accounts, demo accounts, or private accounts.
>
> 2. Occasional opposite position on the same index
> Yes, this can still be considered a violation. FTMO does not distinguish the rule based on whether the opposite exposure was intentional or accidental. The key point is that opposite exposure exists across different accounts. For this reason, we strongly recommend avoiding such overlaps entirely.
>
> 3. Does the rule apply during the Evaluation Process?
> Yes. The Forbidden Trading Practices apply throughout all account stages, including the FTMO Challenge, Verification, and FTMO Account.
>
> 4. Correlated instruments
> Yes, cross-hedging can also involve correlated instruments, not only the exact same symbol. For example, long DAX on one account and short Dow Jones or Nasdaq on another can still create opposing directional exposure.
> We cannot provide a fixed list of correlated instruments, because correlations can change over time. However, the indices you mentioned are clearly related and can be directionally correlated.
>
> 5. Copy trading in the same direction
> Yes, copying your own strategy in the same direction is generally allowed.
> You may copy trades between your own FTMO accounts, between FTMO and your private account, or between FTMO and accounts at another prop firm, provided that no prohibited hedging is created and the applicable maximum capital allocation is respected.
> For FTMO accounts, the standard maximum capital allocation is 400,000 USD per trader or strategy before Scaling. If the same strategy is used across multiple FTMO accounts, the combined allocation must remain within this limit.
>
> 6. What happens if opposite exposure occurs by mistake?
> We cannot provide a hypothetical threshold for what amount of overlap, duration, or size would be considered acceptable.
> If opposite exposure exists across different accounts, this may constitute a Forbidden Trading Practice and can result in corrective action, including account termination, depending on the circumstances and severity.
> For this reason, if you want to remain fully compliant, the safest approach is exactly what you suggested: avoid having opposite exposure across different accounts altogether at any time.
>
